# Controlled Evaluation of Trapping-Set Screening in QC-LDPC Codes

**Research Portfolio | Algorithm Evaluation | Graph Analysis | Scientific Computing | Statistical Analysis**

**Research status:** Manuscript in preparation.

## Project Overview

Low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes are used in reliable digital communication. Their performance can be affected by small structures in the associated Tanner graph, including trapping sets.

This research examines how **structural screening rules for irregular quasi-cyclic LDPC (QC-LDPC) codes** should be evaluated. A central challenge is that codes passing and failing a screen may also differ in other graph characteristics. Without controlling those differences, the observed performance gap may not isolate the association being studied.

The project combines graph-based screening, communication-channel simulation, and statistically controlled comparisons to study this evaluation problem.

## Research Question

How does the observed relationship between a trapping-set screening rule and decoding performance change when relevant Tanner-graph characteristics are controlled?

## Research Approach

- **Structural analysis:** Examine trapping-set screening in relation to Tanner-graph properties, including girth and short cycles.
- **Controlled experimental design:** Use structurally comparable code groups and matched-pair comparisons to reduce identifiable imbalances.
- **Computational simulation:** Evaluate decoding behavior across selected communication-channel conditions.
- **Statistical analysis:** Summarize frame error rate (FER) comparisons and characterize uncertainty while accounting for paired observations.
- **Reproducibility:** Maintain defined simulation settings, predetermined selection procedures, and integrity checks for research records.

## Research Insights

The study illustrates why uncontrolled pass/fail comparisons can give a misleading impression of the strength of a screening rule. Structural comparability is important when interpreting observed FER differences.

These findings describe **associations in a specific simulated setting**. They should not be interpreted as proof of a causal effect, a new decoding algorithm, or a characterization of the classical error-floor regime.
